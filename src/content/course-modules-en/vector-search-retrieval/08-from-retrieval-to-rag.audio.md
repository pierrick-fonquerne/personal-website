Chapter eight. From retrieval to RAG. We can retrieve the right passages, now it is time to turn them into a real answer.

In the previous chapter, we married the two worlds of search: meaning, captured by vectors, and the letter, pinned down by BM twenty-five, fused by rank to stay robust. So we can now retrieve the right passages, whatever the shape of the query. But a frustration has been building for eight chapters: when you ask your store a real question, you do not want a pile of documents to read yourself, you want an answer, written, and grounded on those documents. Retrieving is not answering. This final chapter takes that step.

Let us start with an image. Picture an exceptional candidate at a major oral exam. Vast knowledge, perfect ease, but they sit the exam empty-handed, not a single note allowed. On a general-knowledge question, they shine. Ask them the exact amount of your invoice number eight thousand eight hundred and thirty-one, and they are lost: that information was never part of what they learned. A language model on its own is that candidate.

It suffers from three gaps. First, its knowledge is frozen: it was trained on data cut off at a certain date, and everything after that escapes it. Second, it ignores your private documents: your personal store, your invoices, your notes, none of that was in its training. Third, and most dangerous, it fills the gaps: rather than admit ignorance, it produces a plausible answer, stated with the same confidence as a correct one. This is hallucination, a structural flaw of training, which pushes the model to always say something, even when it should say it does not know. A foundation model is therefore wonderful on the general, and blind on your particular. The problem is not that it is dim, it is brilliant. The problem is that its notes have been confiscated.

Hence the idea of RAG, retrieval-augmented generation. What if, just before letting the model answer, we went and fetched from your store the passages that speak to the question, and laid them under its eyes? It would no longer answer from memory, but by reading. Three stages. First stage, retrieve: we build the context by keeping the k best passages returned by the previous chapter's hybrid search. Second stage, assemble: we write a prompt that contains the question and those passages. Third stage, generate: we hand that prompt to the model, which composes the answer. The remarkable point is the first stage: the retrieval in RAG is nothing other than the search we built throughout the course. RAG does not replace what we learned, it crowns it.

But handing over the passages is not enough, we must also require that the model use them, and only them. This is grounding: the answer must rest on the provided context, not on the model's internal memory, and it must cite its sources so they can be checked. Then comes the question that makes all the difference: what happens when the context does not contain the answer? The model's temptation is to fill the void. The right behavior is the opposite: say so. Answering, I cannot find this information in your documents, is a good answer, not a failure. It is the direct remedy to hallucination: we no longer ask the model to know, we ask it to read, to cite, and to admit when the source is missing. And this has a heavy consequence: the quality of the answer is now capped by the quality of the retrieval. If the right passage is not retrieved, or if it does not make it into the context, the most grounded model in the world can only refuse.

That is exactly what the component puts into your hands. It runs the full pipeline on a small personal store. You pick a query, then switch between the model alone, which answers from memory, and the model plus RAG, which first reads the retrieved passages. In RAG mode, a lever sets the context budget, the number of passages we allow into the model's view. Have fun squeezing it. You will see a beautiful case: on a query whose answer exists in the store, the right passage comes out at rank two, just behind a distractor that speaks of the same topic without carrying the answer. With a budget of one, the model sees only the distractor, and it refuses honestly. Raise the budget to two, the actual invoice enters the context, and the model finally answers, citing its source. The right passage was there all along: only the budget decided whether it entered. Then switch queries, to a question whose answer is nowhere: the model alone invents a date, RAG refuses honestly. Not knowing is here the best answer.

The pipeline we just wired is RAG in its simplest form. Three refinements separate it from real systems. First refinement, chunking. A real document runs forty pages, we do not index it as a single block: we cut it into short pieces, to focus on the right paragraph and fit in the context. Too large, the passage mixes several ideas; too small, it cuts an idea in two. So we make the passages overlap, so that a sentence cut by a boundary is found whole somewhere. The number of passages is about the ceiling of, document length minus passage size, divided by the size minus the overlap, plus one. The larger the overlap, the more passages we produce: the safety is paid in index volume.

Second refinement, the context window. The lever we handled is bounded by a hard constraint of the model: the maximum amount of text it can read at once, measured in tokens. We therefore cannot pour the whole store into the prompt, only the best passages. It is this ceiling that makes the retrieval ranking decisive: if the right passage comes out at rank twelve and the budget only holds eight, it is lost. From this comes reranking: we first let the fast retrieval rake wide, some fifty candidates, then a finer, more expensive model reorders them so the very best take the top spots, the ones that will fit in the window.

Third refinement, agentic retrieval. So far, the pipeline is a one-way trip: one query, one search, one answer. But some questions demand several searches. The idea is to give the model search not as a fixed step, but as a tool it wields itself, as many times as it deems useful. This is the ReAct loop: reason about what is missing, act by searching, observe the passages returned, then start again, until it can conclude. Retrieval becomes dynamic, driven by the model. This is the bridge to what we now call agents.

And a blunt truth, to close on this point: RAG creates no knowledge. All it does is ground an answer on what the retrieval managed to find. If your store does not contain the answer, or if your search does not surface it high enough, no model will guess it. Nothing good in, nothing good out. This is why this course spent seven chapters on retrieval before reaching RAG: the weak link of an answering system is almost never the model, it is the search that feeds it.

The chapter ends with two pen-and-paper exercises, solved step by step: counting the number of passages of a chunking from the size and the overlap, and explaining through the ranks why RAG refuses with a budget of one but answers with a budget of two.

In one sentence. RAG turns a search into an answer: it retrieves the best passages via the course's hybrid search, slips them into the model's bounded context, and asks it to compose an answer grounded on them while citing its sources, or to refuse honestly when the source is missing, so that the quality of the answer stays capped by that of the retrieval.

The closing word. Here we are at the end of the thread. Think back over the path: we started from an almost philosophical idea, meaning as a position in space, and built, step after step, everything needed to make it a real engine. Represent a text as a vector, measure proximity, bypass exhaustive comparison with HNSW, place that choice within the landscape of approximate indexes, give ourselves a differential oracle to verify without fooling ourselves, make the index durable, marry it with the lexical side, and today wire all of it to a language model. Each chapter answered a limit left by the previous one, and this last one closes the loop: RAG only exists because everything else exists first. The sovereign engine I am building will keep evolving, it will become the memory of a personal assistant that reads my own documents to answer me. But the essential is now in your hands: you know what hides behind these buzzwords, not the marketing version, the version that compiles. Thank you for walking this path.
