Research journal. Learning without retraining: a reading note. A frozen model that learns new classes with a plain memory, and what that changes for my page on structural plasticity.

I read a paper by Narayanan, Majumdar and Parbhoo, from Imperial College London, titled Continual Learning without Continual Training. It argues the opposite of what I am looking for, and that is exactly why I read it: to see what I would have to beat.

Here is what the paper shows. A model is trained once, then frozen. After that, to learn new classes, you simply store a few examples in a memory that the model rereads at every prediction. No weight moves, so nothing can be overwritten. Forgetting means the loss of accuracy on what the model already knew. On streams where the domain changes, it is almost zero: minus zero point zero zero seven on Rotated MNIST, and plus zero point zero zero five on chest X-rays from four hospitals. On Split MNIST, where classes arrive in batches, final accuracy reaches zero point seven nine four, against zero point seven four nine for DER plus plus, and zero point seven one four for InfLoRA. But there is still real forgetting of zero point zero seven two on that same stream, because the stored examples interfere with each other. And on Rotated MNIST, accuracy is only zero point seven eight nine, against zero point nine five eight for InfLoRA.

What I take from it is that this paper is a counterpoint to my page on structural plasticity. I defend the idea that a network must change shape to learn. They show you can get almost no forgetting without changing anything, with a plain memory. So this sets an honest baseline: before I say a plastic mechanism helps, it has to beat memory alone. Their measures and their equal-memory protocol, I take as they are.

Now, what I don't buy. It is a preprint, not peer reviewed. The test bench is small: MNIST and X-rays, with at most ten classes. Their claim that their method forgets the least everywhere is contradicted by their own table, since InfLoRA does better twice. There is no test without the annotated concepts. The reference model does no better than a nearest neighbour on pixels, which I measured at zero point six four five on Split MNIST. And the phrase without continual training hides sixteen point four million image draws in the initial training. Finally, the code is missing, and so is the cost when memory grows.

One question stays open. What are the stored vectors worth when the encoder keeps learning? That is where my work and this paper meet. The experiment I want to run: measure accuracy as a function of the number of examples kept, first for memory alone, then for a first plastic mechanism.

In one sentence. A frozen model with a plain memory forgets almost nothing on simple streams, so that is the baseline any plastic mechanism must beat before I say it learns better.
