Within a few days in September, four papers from four different teams told me the same thing. A network does not only keep a trace by filing it somewhere. It also keeps it by changing the shape of its own dynamics. When an idea comes back from four directions, it deserves more than a margin note.

## Slowing down what agrees

Javadzadeh, Hennequin and colleagues record two mouse visual areas at once, V1 and LM, and perturb them with optogenetics ([Nature Neuroscience](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02437-3)). The reciprocal connections between the two areas slow the decay of the patterns that encode the stimulus congruently, and speed up the decay of the others. My reading: the network chooses what to keep by tuning decay rates.

## Shifting the attractor

In the neuron-astrocyte network of Vedovati et al. ([arXiv 2609.16217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16217)), reward triggers a bifurcation that moves a stable attractor to another region of state space. Two mechanisms combine. Uncertainty about the context builds up until the dynamics switch. And the attractor is shallow, with a depth set by the entropy of the environment, which makes behavior viscous.

## Stretching the manifold

In juvenile zebrafish ([Nature Neuroscience](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02429-3)), learning an odor shifts and stretches its manifold in the homolog of the olfactory cortex. The accompanying model draws a sharp line: balanced excitatory and inhibitory assemblies partly generalize to a related odor, an attractor regime does not. My reading: the attractor completes toward the learned state and crushes the variation that would have carried generalization.

## Crossing the edge of chaos

Vaidya gives a mean-field account of what happens to a recurrent network while it learns ([arXiv 2609.19288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19288)). Feedback grows to a critical strength and drives the network from a chaotic regime to stable, task-specific dynamics. The edge of chaos then stops being a setting you pick once: it is a crossing, with a computable critical time.

## What this changes for the living and persistent neuron

The journal and the signature remain the core of the model. But the question is no longer only where the trace is kept: it is also what shape the dynamics took. So I will measure decay rates, attractor shifts and distances between manifolds. One safeguard comes before any conclusion. McKenzie ([arXiv 2609.21272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21272)) proposes to separate a gain change, a displacement within the manifold and a displacement off the manifold, and states when they can be told apart. Without that test, "the neuron persists" stays indistinguishable from "the neuron amplifies".

## Still open

The zebrafish result suggests the useful regime sits before the attractor. The open question is whether a more expressive neuron stays there more easily than a network whose topology was designed by hand. That is the next experiment, on a reservoir first.
