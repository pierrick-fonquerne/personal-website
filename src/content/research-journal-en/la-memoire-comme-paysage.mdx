In a single week, four papers that never cite each other told me the same thing. A network does not keep a trace by filing it somewhere. It keeps it by changing the shape of its own dynamics. When an idea comes back from four independent directions, it deserves more than a margin note.

## Slowing down what agrees

Javadzadeh, Hennequin and colleagues record two mouse visual areas at once, V1 and LM, and perturb them with optogenetics ([Nature Neuroscience](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02437-3)). The reciprocal connections copy nothing. They slow the decay of the patterns both areas agree on, and speed up the decay of the others. The network chooses what to keep by tuning decay rates.

## Shifting the attractor

In the neuron–astrocyte network of Vedovati et al. ([arXiv 2609.16217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16217)), reward triggers a bifurcation that moves a stable attractor to another region of state space. That attractor is shallow, and this is what makes behavior viscous: uncertainty builds up before the dynamics switch to a new context.

## Stretching the manifold

In juvenile zebrafish ([Nature Neuroscience](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02429-3)), learning an odor shifts and stretches its manifold in the olfactory cortex. The accompanying model draws a sharp line: balanced excitatory and inhibitory assemblies generalize to a related odor, an attractor regime does not. The attractor completes toward the learned state and crushes the variation that would have carried generalization.

## Crossing the edge of chaos

Vaidya gives a mean-field account of what happens to a recurrent network while it learns ([arXiv 2609.19288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19288)). Feedback grows to a critical strength and drives the network from a chaotic regime to stable, task-specific dynamics. The edge of chaos is no longer a setting you pick once: it is a crossing, with a computable critical time.

## What this changes for the stateful neuron

I stop looking for where the trace is stored. I measure the shape of the landscape: decay rates, attractor shifts, distances between manifolds. One safeguard comes before any conclusion. McKenzie ([arXiv 2609.21272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21272)) shows that a mere gain change can mimic a change of state. Without that test, "the neuron persists" stays indistinguishable from "the neuron amplifies".

## Still open

The zebrafish result suggests the useful regime sits before the attractor. The open question is whether a more expressive neuron stays there more easily than a network whose topology was designed by hand. That is the next experiment, on a reservoir first.
