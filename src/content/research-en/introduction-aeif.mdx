A child does not read ten billion words to learn what fire is. It reaches out toward a flame, feels the pain, and builds knowledge anchored in its own lived experience: timestamped, emotional, sensory, irreducibly its own. AEIF asks a simple, vertiginous question: could an intelligence be built that way too?

## The problem with LLMs

Large language models are impressive, but their intelligence rests on a particular bet that we have to face squarely.

- They predict **tokens**, not **concepts**: they estimate the most likely next word, without necessarily manipulating the idea behind it.
- Once trained, they are **frozen**: their weights no longer move, they no longer learn from their interactions.
- Their personality is **simulated by a prompt**: we dictate a role to them, they did not become it.
- They have **no body and no grounding**: their words are tied to no sensory experience, and lacking any grip on the real, they hallucinate.

Hence the thesis that guides all of AEIF: **intelligence is born of lived experience, not of statistics over corpora.**

## Everything is an event

The heart of AEIF is a shift in perspective. **Event sourcing**, usually a mere software pattern, becomes here a **cognitive** mechanism.

Each experience becomes an immutable, timestamped, typed **event**. That event never disappears: it is stored in **episodic memory**, it consolidates **semantic memory**, it modifies the **neural network**, it colors the **emotional state**, and it reinforces or weakens **personality traits**.

The unique payoff of this approach: you can **replay** the formation of a belief, a trait, a fear. The "why" of a behavior stops being a black box. It becomes **traceable**.

## The six-layer architecture

AEIF is organized into six layers that communicate **only through events**. None knows the inside of the others: they share only a bus and a protocol. From the bottom up:

- **aei-events**: the shared event protocol and bus, foundation of everything else.
- **aei-neural**: from-scratch neural compute in Rust, with dynamic topology and Hebbian learning.
- **aei-perception** and **aei-motor**: the senses and the body, the interface with the world.
- **aei-memory**: episodic, semantic, procedural and emotional memory, with consolidation, selective forgetting and associative recall.
- **aei-cognition**: reasoning, planning, attention, emotions, motivations and decision.
- **aei-identity**: traits, preferences, values, self-awareness and narrative continuity.

## Learning from surprise

How does such an architecture learn? Through **predictive coding** (Friston). The cycle is clear: **predict**, **observe**, **compare**, then **learn from the error**.

When surprise is **low**, the observation confirms the model, which is **reinforced**. When surprise is **high**, the observation contradicts the model, which must **change**. The crucial point: the learning signal is **intrinsic surprise**, not an external reward as in reinforcement learning. The agent does not wait to be praised, it corrects itself.

## A topology that rewrites itself

The AEIF network has no fixed shape. It practices **neuroplasticity**: neurons and synapses are **created** when new concepts appear, **reinforced** by repetition (Hebb's rule), **pruned** by disuse. It is not only the weights that change: it is the **topology itself** that evolves.

This is where AEIF meets another of my programs. **AEIF provides the substrate**: a network where every structural mutation is an immutable, traced, replayable event. But its mutations stay **random**: it knows how to mutate, not yet how to choose. The missing engine, those local rules that decide what to grow and what to let die, is the subject of the **SOAG** program. To dig into this junction, see the shared concept of [structural plasticity](/en/research/plasticite-structurelle).

## A minimal formalization

Let us state things cleanly. A **lived event** is a triple $e = (t, \tau, \rho)$: a timestamp $t$, a type $\tau$, and a payload $\rho$.

The **agent state** at time $t$ is a fold over the event history, starting from an initial state $S_0$:

$$
S_t = \mathrm{fold}(S_0, \{e_i : t_i \le t\})
$$

Learning is driven by **surprise**, the gap between observation $o$ and prediction $\hat{o}$:

$$
\sigma = \lVert o - \hat{o} \rVert
$$

When $\sigma$ is small, the model is confirmed and reinforced; when $\sigma$ is large, the network must change.

## Where AEIF sits

AEIF is not alone in seeking a grounded intelligence. Placing it against two other families brings out its own bet.

| Property       | LLMs                     | World Models (JEPA)                | AEIF                            |
| -------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Source         | massive text corpora     | real-world data                    | lived events                    |
| Learning       | frozen pre-training      | self-supervised on video+physics   | continuous, by experience       |
| Architecture   | static transformer       | designed JEPA hierarchy            | dynamic networks from scratch   |
| Memory         | context window           | persistent                         | event-sourced, multi-type       |
| Personality    | simulated by a prompt    | not addressed                      | emergent from experience        |
| Grounding      | none                     | physical                           | sensorimotor and emotional      |

Each column is a coherent bet. AEIF's is the least explored of the three.

## Limits and open questions

It would be dishonest to present all this as solved. The open work is plentiful.

- **Representation**: neural only, or hybrid neural-symbolic?
- **Quantifying surprise**: which metric holds, and over which space?
- **Forming hypotheses**: how does the agent generate them, and how does it test them?
- **Consolidating or forgetting**: when to engrave a memory, when to let it fade?
- **What to predict, and when**: over which modality and at what horizon?
- **Grounding concepts**: how to tie an abstract idea to the experience that founds it?
- **Time and causality**: how to reason about the order and the causes of events?
- **Emergence of personality**: how does a trait really arise from events? This is the final goal.
