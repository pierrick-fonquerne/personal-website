Early flight imitated the flapping wings of birds: ornithopters that ran on brute force and superficial mimicry. Efficient flight came only once we identified the principle of lift (a fixed wing, aerodynamics), which owes nothing to imitating biology and everything to understanding why it works. Today's language models may be in the flapping-wings phase: they work, but by brute force, stacking parameters the way we once stacked engine power onto inefficient designs. The motive question of SOAG is simple: what is the lift principle of intelligence?

## The founding hypothesis

The hypothesis fits in one sentence, and I put it in bold because everything else follows from it: **the brain computes only what is necessary, where it is necessary, when it is necessary.** Sparsity, spikes, locality, plasticity, inhibition and oscillations are not independent mechanisms: they are all consequences of this single principle. A language model does the exact opposite: it computes everything, everywhere, all the time.

The gap is easier to see than to describe. Run both modes below and watch the cumulative cost diverge: the dense mode pays for the whole network at every step, the sparse mode pays only for the stimulated neighborhood.

## The 1969 bifurcation

History clarifies the bet. In **1943**, McCulloch and Pitts proposed the logical, stateless neuron: a gate that sees the present and nothing else. In **1949**, Hebb formulated his rule, neurons that fire together wire together. In **1958**, Rosenblatt built the perceptron, a machine that learns. Then in **1969**, Minsky and Papert proved that the perceptron cannot learn XOR, triggering the first AI winter.

The field then split. **Response 1**: add layers. That is backpropagation (1986), then deep learning (2012), then large models. **Response 2**: enrich the component itself. That is STDP (1998), self-organized spiking networks, and SOAG. **Response 3**, later: a designed world model (LeCun, 2022, JEPA).

## Enriching the component

This is SOAG's first axis. A poor neuron, McCulloch-Pitts style, is **stateless**: it sees only the present instant, and the same input always yields the same output. A rich neuron instead carries **internal state** over time (membrane potential, leak, adaptive threshold, energy), and its meaning emerges from collective interaction. The same input train produces, in the rich neuron, a **temporal integration** that the poor one simply cannot express.

Below, inject pulses too weak to fire alone. The poor neuron never reacts. The rich one integrates pulse after pulse until it crosses its threshold and spikes.

## The six axes

The program explores six complementary axes. The first four form the irreducible core (SOAG-v0).

- **(1) Redefine the neuron**: a unit with rich internal state (potential, adaptive threshold, energy), not a mere weighted sum followed by a nonlinearity.
- **(2) Redefine the synapse** as the main locus of processing: typed, with its own temporal dynamics, governed by purely local learning rules (STDP, three-factor learning).
- **(3) Inhibition** as a first-class computational mechanism, needed for sparsity, competitive selection and dynamic stability.
- **(4) Stability mechanisms**: local constraints (energy budget, homeostasis, mortality, forgetting) that prevent divergence without any global control.

Two extensions complete the core.

- **(5) Emergent spatial organization**: a continuous functional space where distance shapes connectivity and clusters emerge from activity.
- **(6) Multi-scale temporal dynamics**: each component with its own time constant, from the spike (milliseconds) to structural reorganization (hours or days).

## The missing engine

Here is the link with my other work. **SOAG looks for the missing engine** of structural plasticity: the local rules that decide what to grow and what to let die, with no central control. The relationship is sharp: AEIF provides a traceable substrate that mutates at random, and SOAG aims to be the criterion that orients those mutations.

This engine is exactly the hinge described in the cross-cutting concept of [structural plasticity](/en/research/plasticite-structurelle).

## A minimal formalization

Let us state things cleanly. A local learning rule depends only on the relative timing of two spikes (STDP):

$$
\Delta w = f(t_{\text{post}} - t_{\text{pre}})
$$

Stability comes from a local energy budget rather than a global loss. Each unit spends to fire and must stay under a budget:

$$
\sum_{k} a_k \le B
$$

where $a_k$ is the activity of unit $k$ and $B$ the budget. One sentence captures the spirit: nothing here reads a global error signal, every decision is local.

## What SOAG draws

What this program draws no longer looks like a neural network in the classical sense. It is a **computational ecosystem**: a distributed, event-driven adaptive system with plastic topology and local regulation. Four implications follow.

- Intelligence is an emergent behavior, not an optimized function.
- Architecture is a result, not a choice.
- Time is the medium of computation, not a dimension of representation.
- Memory is a dynamic, not a storage.

The program explores how far this metaphor can go, and where it stops being productive.

## A personal note

> Personal note (draft to rewrite): SOAG is my most speculative bet, and betting on the branch almost everyone left behind is uncomfortable. I am not sure it leads anywhere. But the questions this program forces me to ask (why does the brain spend twenty watts and a model a hundred megawatts?) are exactly the ones that keep me up at night and keep me reading. As long as the question stays honest, I would rather dig into it than run from it.
